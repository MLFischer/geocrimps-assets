# GeoCrimp #048 — Caption

In the Grand Canyon you can put a hand flat on a billion years of nothing.

Layers stack in order, oldest at the bottom. But the record only exists where sediment was piling up. Where the land was lifted and stripped instead, nothing was recorded — and when deposition finally resumed, the new beds were laid straight onto a much older, eroded surface. That contact is an unconformity, and it stores time the way a gap in a diary does.

Above the Granite Gorge, the Great Unconformity puts roughly 525-million-year-old Tapeats Sandstone directly onto Vishnu basement rocks of about 1.75 billion years. The National Park Service puts the missing interval at approximately 1.2 billion years across that single contact — around a quarter of Earth's entire history, in a line you can touch.

Fun fact: it isn't always one surface. Where the tilted Grand Canyon Supergroup survives in between, the gap splits in two — the Great Nonconformity below it, worth up to 500 million years, and the Great Angular Unconformity above it, worth up to 750. The famous single line is what you get where the Supergroup is missing and the two erosion surfaces come together.

Source: Missing Time at Grand Canyon National Park, National Park Service · Karlstrom, Crossey, Mathis & Bowman (2021), Telling Time at Grand Canyon: 2020 Update.
