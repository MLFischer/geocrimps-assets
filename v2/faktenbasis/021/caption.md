# GeoCrimp #021 — Caption

Ice ages keep a beat — and it's written in Earth's orbit.

Three slow wobbles change how sunlight reaches the planet: the shape of Earth's orbit (eccentricity, ~100,000 years), the tilt of its axis (obliquity, ~41,000 years) and its slow spin-wobble (precession, ~23,000 years). Over the last million years, the ~100,000-year beat has dominated — long, gradual freezes ended by fast melts.

Fun fact: in 1976, Hays, Imbrie and Shackleton found those exact orbital periods hidden in deep-sea sediment cores. Their paper's title said it all: 'Pacemaker of the Ice Ages.'

Source: Hays, Imbrie & Shackleton (1976), Science · EPICA ice-core record.
