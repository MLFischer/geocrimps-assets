# GeoCrimp #016 — Caption

The towers of the Elbe Sandstone weren't built up. They're what's left behind.

Thick beds of Cretaceous sandstone were laid down almost perfectly flat. Later uplift and the Lausitz Thrust cracked the plate along vertical joints — a grid of fractures through horizontal rock. Water crept into those joints and widened them over millions of years, stripping away everything but the most resistant cores: the free-standing towers and flat-topped table mountains climbers know.

Fun fact: those flat summits are the original bedding surface. The Lilienstein, Pfaffenstein and Königstein are all remnants of one continuous sandstone plate.

Source: Geologie des Elbsandsteingebirges (LfULG Sachsen) · regional geology, Sächsische Schweiz.
