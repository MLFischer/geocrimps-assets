# GeoCrimp #027 — Caption

There is a whole food web down there that has never seen the sun.

Along mid-ocean ridges, cold seawater seeps into cracks in young, hot ocean crust. Close to the magma it is heated to roughly 400 °C and strips metals and sulphide out of the rock on the way. When that fluid jets back into near-freezing seawater, the minerals crash out as a dark plume and pile up into chimneys — black smokers. The animals around them don't live on sunlight at all: microbes get their energy from the chemistry of the vent fluid, and tube worms, crabs and clams live off those microbes. Chemosynthesis instead of photosynthesis.

Fun fact: the submersible Alvin found the first vent communities on the Galápagos Rift in February 1977. The first true black smoker was spotted about two years later, on the East Pacific Rise.

Source: Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution, history of hydrothermal vent discovery · Galápagos Rift 1977 · vent fluid temperatures ~350–400 °C.
