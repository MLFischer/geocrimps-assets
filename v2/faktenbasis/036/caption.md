# GeoCrimp #036 — Caption

There is no new rock. Everything you have ever pulled on has been something else before.

Magma cools and crystals grow into each other: igneous rock. Weather takes it apart into grains, rivers stack the grains in layers, and burial cements them: sedimentary rock. Squeeze and heat that, and the minerals regrow and line up: metamorphic rock. Push it deeper still and it melts, and the loop starts again. The cycle has no fixed route either — a sedimentary rock can be eroded straight back into sediment without ever seeing a magma chamber.

Fun fact: quartz is the great survivor of the whole system. It resists chemical weathering better than almost anything else in a granite, which is why so much of the world's sand is quartz — grains that have been round the loop more than once.

Source: Winter, Principles of Igneous and Metamorphic Petrology · rock cycle, standard petrology.
