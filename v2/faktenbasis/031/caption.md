# GeoCrimp #031 — Caption

Those glassy glacier-polished slabs weren't just worn smooth. Something was added.

Glacial polish used to be explained as pure abrasion — grit at the base of a glacier sanding bedrock down. That part is real; the striations are scratch marks. But work on Yosemite's polished surfaces showed the shine is also a distinct layer sitting on top of the rock, only micrometres thick. Subglacial water dissolves the ground-up rock flour and re-precipitates it, and the resulting coating is strongly depleted in base cations and enriched in silica compared with the bedrock beneath it. Polish is something deposited, not only something removed.

Fun fact: that film is also why the polish is still there at all. It is almost impermeable — it sheds water fast, which keeps lichen and moss from taking hold and slows chemical weathering. The shine outlives the glacier by thousands of years.

Source: Siman-Tov et al. (2017), 'The coating layer of glacial polish', Geology 45 · follow-up on composition and formation age of the amorphous silica coating, Geology 47 (2019).
