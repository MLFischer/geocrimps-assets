# GeoCrimp #034 — Caption

Almost the whole Mid-Atlantic Ridge is two kilometres under water. In Iceland you can walk into it.

A mid-ocean ridge is a spreading centre: two plates pulling apart, magma rising into the gap and freezing there as new crust. Along this stretch the supply is far larger than normal — usually attributed to a hot spot beneath the ridge — and the whole thing has built itself right up above sea level. Continuous GPS measurements give a full spreading rate of about 19 to 20 millimetres a year — roughly two centimetres. At Þingvellir the pulling is visible as open fissures and a dropped block of ground you can walk straight down into.

Fun fact: the popular line about standing with one foot on each plate doesn't survive the data. The pulling is shared across a plate boundary zone roughly 100 to 150 kilometres wide, not one tidy crack.

Source: Geirsson et al. (2006), 'Current plate movements across the Mid-Atlantic Ridge determined from 5 years of continuous GPS measurements in Iceland', Journal of Geophysical Research: Solid Earth.
