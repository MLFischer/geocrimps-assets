# GeoCrimp #018 — Caption

Stand on the Zugspitze and you're standing on a coral reef.

Germany's highest peak is Wetterstein limestone — a thick, very pure carbonate that grew as a reef and lagoon in the warm Triassic Tethys sea, about 235–240 million years ago. Corals, sponges and algae built the platform; deep burial turned it to solid rock; then the Alpine collision thrust that ancient seafloor thousands of metres into the sky.

Fun fact: the Wetterstein limestone runs up to a kilometre thick and is 95–98% carbonate — a reef so vast it builds whole mountains.

Source: Ortner et al., The Zugspitze cross section (N. Calcareous Alps) · geology of the Wetterstein/Zugspitze.
