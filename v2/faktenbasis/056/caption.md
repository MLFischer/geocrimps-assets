# GeoCrimp #056 — Caption

Every wall with layers in it can be read, and the rule takes one sentence.

Each bed of sedimentary rock is older than the one above it and younger than the one below it. That is superposition, and the National Park Service calls it the most basic principle of relative dating. It works because of a second rule that has to come first: original horizontality — strata are always initially deposited in nearly horizontal positions. Flat is the starting condition.

Put the two together and a tilted wall becomes a statement about time. If beds are always laid down near-horizontal, then a bed standing at a steep angle must have been moved into that position by crustal disturbances after it was deposited. The rock records the deposition and the deformation, in that order, and you can see which came second.

Fun fact: the NPS credits Nicolaus Steno in 1669 with the first clear statement that strata show sequential changes — that rocks have histories. He worked it out in the mountains of western Italy. No dates, no isotopes, no fossils needed: just the observation that things land on top of other things.

Source: Geologic Principles — Superposition and Original Horizontality, U.S. National Park Service.
