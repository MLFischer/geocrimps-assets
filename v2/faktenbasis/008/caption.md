# GeoCrimp #008 — Caption

Two-finger pockets, monos, huecos — nobody carved them. They dissolved.

Rain picks up CO₂ from the air and the soil and turns into a weak acid. Trickling over limestone for thousands of years, it slowly dissolves the rock — and it attacks the weakest spots first: fossils, burrows, fine fractures. Wherever the chemistry wins, a pocket opens.

Fun fact: water seeping through soil can carry far more CO₂ than rain picks up from the air — the ground itself sharpens the acid.

Source: Ford & Williams (2007), Karst Hydrogeology and Geomorphology.
