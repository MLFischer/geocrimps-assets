# GeoCrimp #015 — Caption

Fontainebleau's golden slopers have one rule: never climb them wet.

Font sandstone wears a thin, rock-hard silica crust over much softer sand. Dry, that crust is glassy and strong. But when rain soaks in, the water cancels the capillary tension that helps hold the grains together and weakens the rock at the tips of tiny cracks — a hold that survived millennia can shear off in a single damp session. And because the crust forms only slowly, a broken hold never grows back.

Fun fact: this is why Font's local ethic is strict — give the rock a day or two to dry. Climbing it wet doesn't just risk a fall; it permanently destroys the boulder.

Source: Thiry & Maréchal (2001), Journal of Sedimentary Research · Duda & Renner (2013), Geophysical Journal International — water weakening of sandstone (incl. Fontainebleau sandstone) · Fischer & Krause, Klettergeologie, Boulder Mag 02/2026.
