# GeoCrimp #051 — Caption

Peak District gritstone is a granite highland, taken apart and rebuilt.

Around 320 million years ago, in the Namurian, big rivers ran south into northern England and built deltas out across the basin. The British Geological Survey describes the pattern as upward-coarsening cycles: finer material dropped first, then coarser sand as the river mouth pushed closer. Stack those cycles and you get the Millstone Grit. What makes it grit rather than ordinary sandstone is the grain: BGS calls coarse-grained to granular, feldspathic sandstones the dominant and characteristic component, made of detrital quartz and potassium feldspar with minor plagioclase and mica.

That feldspar is the tell. Pure quartz sand is what you get when sand has been worked over long enough for everything else to break down. Grit still carries its feldspar, which is why it is classed as subarkose rather than clean quartz arenite.

Fun fact: the sediment did not come from England. Provenance work and zircon ages point to a granitic and gneissic terrain to the north — probably Scotland and Scandinavia. Every edge in the Peak is imported highland.

Source: Baseline Report Series 18 — The Millstone Grit of Northern England, British Geological Survey / Environment Agency.
