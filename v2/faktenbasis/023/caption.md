# GeoCrimp #023 — Caption

The boulders at Chironico are the wreckage of one very bad day.

Chironico, in Ticino's Valle Leventina, sits on a landslide deposit. About 13,400 years ago — roughly three thousand years after the glaciers had pulled out of the valley — around 530 million cubic metres of granite gneiss let go of the eastern valley wall and slid down along foliation planes that dipped toward the valley. The mass ran across the valley floor and dammed the Ticino river. Cosmogenic exposure ages from ten of the blocks, together with runout modelling, point to one single collapse rather than the two events that had been assumed before.

Fun fact: it is one of the few pre-Holocene landslides known in the Alps — and the oldest dated one in crystalline rock.

Source: Claude et al. (2014), "The Chironico landslide (Valle Leventina, southern Swiss Alps): age and evolution", Swiss Journal of Geosciences 107, 273–291.
