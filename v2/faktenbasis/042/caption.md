# GeoCrimp #042 — Caption

There is a rock that records the moment the planet started breathing.

For most of Earth's early history there was no free oxygen. Iron from volcanoes and weathering stayed dissolved in seawater, because dissolved iron stays in solution until an oxidant turns up. Then cyanobacteria worked out photosynthesis and began releasing oxygen — long before the air itself changed. Where that oxygen met the dissolved iron, the iron turned into insoluble oxide and sank. What settled is banded iron formation: alternating layers of iron oxide and silica, red and dark, stacked in stripes. The atmosphere only followed later: from about 2.4 billion years ago free oxygen accumulated in sea and air, the Great Oxidation Event. Most of the banded iron was already on the sea floor by then: alternating layers of iron oxide and silica, red and dark, stacked in stripes.

Fun fact: these rocks are not forming anywhere today. The oceans have oxygen now, so iron can no longer build up dissolved in seawater. Banded iron belongs to the deep past — and it is where most of the world's iron ore comes from.

Source: Great Oxidation Event and banded iron formations, standard Precambrian geology · BIF composition: alternating iron oxide (hematite, magnetite) and chert layers.
