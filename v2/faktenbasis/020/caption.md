# GeoCrimp #020 — Caption

Drop Mount Everest into the deepest ocean and it still drowns.

Everest rises 8,849 metres above sea level — the highest point on land, still being pushed up where India collides with Asia. But the Challenger Deep, in the Mariana Trench, plunges about 10,935 metres down, where one plate subducts beneath another. Set Everest on that trench floor and its summit would still sit roughly two kilometres beneath the waves.

Fun fact: the deep used to be more exclusive than the Moon — but not any more. Twelve people have walked on the Moon; more than 25 have now reached the bottom of the Challenger Deep, most of them since 2019.

Source: NOAA / GEBCO bathymetry · Everest height: 2020 China–Nepal survey (8,848.86 m) · descent counts: Guinness World Records (27 people to July 2022).
