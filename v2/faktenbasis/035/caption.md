# GeoCrimp #035 — Caption

An ice core is the only climate archive that hands you the actual air.

Snow falls and buries the snow before it. As the pile compacts, the pores between the grains stay connected to the atmosphere for a while — only about 50 to 100 metres down do they pinch shut into sealed bubbles. From that moment each bubble is a sample of the atmosphere of its time. The EPICA core at Dome C in Antarctica carried that record back roughly 800,000 years: measured greenhouse-gas concentrations, not a proxy for them. One consequence of the slow sealing is that the trapped air is always somewhat younger than the ice around it.

Fun fact: in 2025 the Beyond EPICA drilling reached bedrock at about 2,800 metres and brought up a continuous record reaching back at least 1.2 million years — roughly four times the age of our species. The deepest ice sits below that and is older still; how much older is exactly what the dating has to settle.

Source: EPICA Dome C CO₂ record (Bereiter et al. 2015, Geophysical Research Letters) · Beyond EPICA — Oldest Ice, British Antarctic Survey project reporting, 2025 bedrock recovery.
