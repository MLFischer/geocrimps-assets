# GeoCrimp #026 — Caption

Desert rock doesn't get carved into pockets. It gets hollowed out from the inside.

Honeycombs — tafoni, alveoli, the huecos you jam a fist into — are largely salt at work. Salty water soaks into a porous rock, the surface dries, the salt crystallises in the pores, and the growing crystals push grains off the wall one at a time. Often a hard crust forms on the outside first, where evaporating water leaves silica, iron oxide or calcite behind. Once that crust is breached, the weaker interior weathers much faster than the shell, and the hollow keeps growing inward. Material is removed from the rock — not carved into it.

Fun fact: the word climbers use comes from Hueco Tanks in Texas, which isn't sandstone at all. It's a syenite intrusion of about 35 million years — pits like these form in granite and basalt just as happily.

Source: honeycomb weathering — salt weathering, case hardening and core softening literature · Hueco Tanks geology (Jackson School of Geosciences, UT Austin).
