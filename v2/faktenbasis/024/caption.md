# GeoCrimp #024 — Caption

Conglomerate is a climbing gym that built itself.

It is old river gravel, glued back together. Rivers and debris flows round the stones and dump them in fans and deltas; later, groundwater precipitates calcite, iron oxide or silica in the gaps and turns the loose pile into rock. The cement is usually weaker than the pebbles it holds, so weathering strips the matrix back and leaves the cobbles standing out of the wall — bolt-on holds, delivered by a river. At Meteora the pinnacles are stacked fan-delta conglomerates of the Mesohellenic basin; at Montserrat outside Barcelona it is an Eocene fan delta of the Ebro basin, over a kilometre of stacked conglomerate carved into towers.

Fun fact: the cement quality changes from route to route. Climbers at Meteora report lines where a single cobble takes your whole body weight — and sections where the stones sit in the wall like pebbles in dried mud. Test before you commit.

Source: Meteora conglomerates (IUGS Geological Heritage site: Oligocene–Miocene molasse and rock pinnacles) · Montserrat conglomerate (middle–late Eocene fan delta, Ebro basin) · sedimentary petrology: clast vs. matrix strength in conglomerate.
