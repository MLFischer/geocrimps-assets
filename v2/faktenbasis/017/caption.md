# GeoCrimp #017 — Caption

Those ripples on a limestone slab? Rain carved them — and now you climb them.

When rain hits bare limestone, the first thin film of water is the most aggressive: before it saturates, it dissolves the rock fastest. Running downslope, it cuts a set of narrow, sharp-ridged channels a few centimetres apart — rillenkarren, or solution flutes. On a slab, the ridges between those flutes are exactly the ripples your fingertips and rubber read.

Fun fact: rillenkarren always run straight downslope and stop where standing water would pool — a built-in record of how water once flowed across the rock.

Source: Ford & Williams (2007), Karst Hydrogeology and Geomorphology · karren (solution flute) literature.
