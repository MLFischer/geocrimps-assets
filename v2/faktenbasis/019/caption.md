# GeoCrimp #019 — Caption

About 22,000 years ago, you could have walked from Britain to mainland Europe.

At the peak of the last ice age, so much water was locked into ice sheets that global sea level sat roughly 120–130 metres below today's. Continental shelves that are now seabed lay exposed as dry land — including 'Doggerland', the plains that joined Britain to the continent across what is now the North Sea.

Fun fact: fishing trawlers still haul mammoth bones and Stone-Age tools off the floor of the North Sea — the drowned remains of Doggerland.

Source: Lambeck et al. (2014), PNAS — sea level from the Last Glacial Maximum to the Holocene.
