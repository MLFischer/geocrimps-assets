# GeoCrimp #022 — Caption

Those stripes in the rock are the reason your feet stay on.

Gneiss is a metamorphic rock whose minerals have been sorted into layers: pale bands of quartz and feldspar, darker bands rich in mica. The layering is called foliation, and it forms deep in the crust, where heat and directed pressure let grains grow and line up instead of sitting in a random mix. At the surface those layers don't weather equally — mica sits near the unstable end of the mineral weathering sequence, quartz at the stable end. So the dark bands retreat first and the pale ones are left standing slightly proud: edges, rails and smears, for free.

Fun fact: the same foliation that hands you footholds is also a ready-made sliding plane. Whole mountainsides come down along it — including the one in tomorrow's post.
